You found me through Southern Legends — or maybe you've been there from the beginning. Either way, this is the letter behind the stories.

The profiles on the site are about other people. This newsletter is about the work — what I notice when I'm sitting across from someone who's been building something, what I carry home, what I'm still trying to figure out.

The site is free. This letter is free. If it's ever not free, you'll know before it happens and the reason will be honest.

Thanks for being here.

Substack · First Issue

Subject: What the Bloom Bar was waiting for

Short version of the Whole of It essay — Bloom Bar origin, the farm, the hospital, the expo. 400 words. Entry point for Substack discovery readers who don't know the backstory.

"My wife and I bought a converted horse trailer for $7,000 while I was manic. We called it the Bloom Bar — a floral design station she could take to markets and weddings. We used it for one spring. Then the flower farm closed and I ended up in a psychiatric hospital. The trailer sat in the grass between our family's two homes for fourteen months.

On October 18 it's the centerpiece of a bridal show at Anniston Museums and Gardens.

It didn't sit there doing nothing. It was waiting."
