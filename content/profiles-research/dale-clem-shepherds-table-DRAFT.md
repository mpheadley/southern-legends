# The Man Who Set the Table

Before the sanctuary opened on Sunday mornings, there was breakfast.

Dale Clem started Shepherd's Table, a weekly breakfast for mostly homeless neighbors, and sometimes low-income families with children, in Anniston. [EDIT: confirm the hosting church and years — tied to Interfaith / a local UMC congregation.] It is a small, ordinary thing. Eggs, coffee, a warm room, a place at a table for people who do not get offered many. It is also exactly the kind of thing that tells you what a person actually believes, as opposed to what they say on a stage.

## What he carries

You cannot tell Dale Clem's story honestly without the hardest part of it, and it is not ours to tell lightly. On Palm Sunday in 1994, an EF-4 tornado tore through Goshen United Methodist Church during worship. Twenty people were killed. Among them was Hannah, the Clems' four-year-old daughter. [EDIT: confirm details and pastoral roles with Dale/Kelly before publish — Rev. Kelly Clem was the Goshen pastor; Dale is also a United Methodist minister.]

Out of that, Dale wrote *Winds of Fury, Circles of Grace*, a search for meaning that never once pretends the meaning was easy or clean. He later wrote *40 Days in the Wilderness: Reflection and Prayers Along the Appalachian Trail*. Both books are the work of a man who walked into grief instead of around it, and came back able to sit with other people in theirs.

That is the throughline. The breakfast table and the books are the same gesture. A man who knows what it is to lose everything does not flinch from people who already have.

## Why he counts as a legend

Southern Legends keeps the record of people who stayed and did the work where they were from. Dale Clem is a witness who limps, which is the only kind worth trusting. He did not come to the homeless of Calhoun County as a rescuer with clean hands. He came as someone who had been broken open and chose to keep showing up with breakfast.
