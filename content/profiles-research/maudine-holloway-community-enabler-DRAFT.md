# The House With Her Name On It

When Anniston finally built its shelter, they put Maudine Holloway's name on part of it. That tells you most of what you need to know.

Holloway is the longtime executive director of Community Enabler Developer, an Anniston nonprofit that has spent years doing the plainest kind of good there is: getting food and clothing to people who do not have enough of either. [EDIT: confirm years of service and founding details with Maudine before publish.] It is not glamorous work. It does not trend. It is the daily, unspectacular labor of making sure a neighbor eats and has a coat, and she has done it long enough that the community decided to carve her name into something permanent.

## Holloway House

Inside Martha's Hope, the low-barrier shelter that United Way of East Central Alabama helped bring to Anniston, there is a transitional living space called the Holloway House. It offers up to forty people, including two family units, a safe place to work toward permanent housing and self-sufficiency, with coordinated care along the way. [EDIT: confirm current capacity and program details.]

Naming it for her was not sentiment. It was accuracy. You name the transitional house after the person who has been transitioning people their whole career.

## Why she counts as a legend

Southern Legends is a record of the people who stayed and did the work where they were from. Maudine Holloway is one of the quiet load-bearing walls of Anniston's care for its poorest neighbors. She belongs in the same paragraph as Lori Floyd, the Interfaith folks, and Martha Vandervoort, whose name the whole shelter carries: a small, stubborn company of people who refuse to quit on the same folks, year after year.
