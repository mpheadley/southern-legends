I was selling bouquets at a Bloom Bar table at the Jacksonville Farmers Market in June of 2025 when Debbi Merrill handed me her card. She was warm about it, the way people are when they've decided to keep going. I didn't know until later that she was about a month into being a widow. ## Sixty Acres in Saks

Blackberry Hill Alpaca Farm sits on sixty acres at 6330 Saks Road, just north of Anniston. There's a pavilion, a farmhouse, and a pond, and the place can hold up to a hundred guests. Debbi and Hank built it together — the website [VERIFY: still] lists Hank as co-owner, which is one of those small, hard artifacts a person doesn't always have the heart to change. The animals are the draw people come for. Sixty acres is what they stay for.

Alpacas are strange, gentle company. They hum. They study you. They don't need anything from you, which is exactly why people who are carrying something heavy tend to go quiet around them. If that sounds familiar, it should — it's the same thing [Redbird Willow Farm](/essays/redbird-willow-farm) discovered about grief and animals a few miles away, and the two farms know each other. Redbird refers venue couples to Blackberry Hill. Two farms in the same county, both run by women who kept the land after loss, both letting other people come stand near the animals and breathe.

## What Kept Going

Here is the part I keep thinking about. Debbi didn't sell. She could have. A sixty-acre farm is a lot to carry alone, and grief is a good enough reason to let a thing go — I know, because I've let a farm go for less. She kept it. When I called her in the spring of 2026 [VERIFY: May 17, 2026] to talk about the bridal show, she was kind and interested and honest that she wasn't fully committed yet. That's the right way to be about a thing you intend to keep.

She's a founding vendor partner for The Aisle now, and the farm is open to couples as a venue — the pavilion for the reception, the pond and the pasture for the photos, the alpacas for the part of the day nobody plans and everybody remembers.
