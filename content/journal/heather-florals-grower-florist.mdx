Full disclosure up front: Heather is my wife. I'd write about her work anyway, because it holds up on its own — but you deserve to know that going in.

Most florists learned flowers in a shop. Heather learned them in a field.

## The Farm Before the Florals

Before Heather Florals, there was Headley Flower Farm — cut flowers grown in Calhoun County and sold at farmers markets, coffee shops, and local events. Brides bought from her. Other florists bought from her. That's the background she carries into every wedding now: she knows what a bloom looks like at the source, which stems survive an Alabama ceremony in July, and which ones won't make it from the arch to the reception.

That's a different kind of knowledge than you get behind a cooler. It's the difference between someone who orders flowers and someone who has grown them, cut them at the right hour, and watched them wilt when they were cut at the wrong one. She's been designing wedding and event florals in Northeast Alabama for over a decade now, with more than a hundred weddings behind her. Her work is garden-style: loose, natural arrangements that look freshly gathered instead of factory-built.

## Small On Purpose

Heather limits her bookings each season. Your wedding doesn't get wedged between six others, and the person who takes your consultation is the same person placing the last stem on your arch. No junior designer, no subcontractor working from someone else's plan. She doesn't hand you a package sheet, either — she builds a proposal from what's actually in season, what's local when it can be, and what looks like you.

She also manages the venue at [Silver Run Chapel](/essays/silver-run-chapel), the 1850 chapel in Munford, so she's seen weddings from both sides: the flowers arriving and the whole day holding together around them.

## The Bloom Bar

If you've heard her name, it's probably because of the Bloom Bar — a flower station set up during a reception, usually at cocktail hour. Guests pick fresh blooms from a curated table and build a small arrangement to take home. It's décor, an activity, and a favor in one setup, and couples keep saying the same thing about it: it was the most talked-about detail of the night. You can add it to a full floral package or book it on its own.

None of it is complicated, which is the point. A grower who kept growing, a florist who limits the calendar so she can be the one who shows up, and a reception table full of flowers people carry home. That's the whole business. It's enough.
