The highest compliment you can give a church musician is that you stopped noticing them.

Not because the playing wasn't good. Because it was good enough that the room stopped fighting itself — the music and the liturgy and the congregation all moving in the same direction, and nobody in the pew thinking about the organ anymore, just the thing the organ was trying to open.

Blair Callaway has been at that bench long enough to have earned that.

---

He is a music educator and instrumentalist on the faculty at Jacksonville State University, where he teaches instrumental music education and Class Piano. He spent 28 years teaching high school band before moving to JSU — Heritage, Foley, Alma Bryant, Russellville, a handful of programs across Alabama and Georgia. He performed with Tara Winds at the Midwest Band and Orchestra Clinic. Alabama Winds at the Western International Band Clinic. He has been past president of the American School Band Directors Association.

That is the resume. The man is something else.

---

At Anniston First United Methodist Church he has played the organ. At Jacksonville First Presbyterian Church he shares the bench with Susie Dempsey — the two of them rotating in, each one holding the room on their Sunday, the congregation not always knowing which one they're about to get but knowing the music will be there.

That arrangement — two musicians, one instrument, one congregation — works when both people are serious about the work and unserious about the credit. That is Blair Callaway.

I have sat in those rooms when he was playing. I have been part of a choir he was accompanying. There is a way a pianist or organist can hold a choir — not driving them, not following them, but finding the breath before the phrase and landing where the section needs to land. Blair does that. He listens first. The playing comes second.

---

He grew up in Ringgold, Georgia. He holds degrees from JSU and the University of North Alabama. He has been in Northeast Alabama long enough to be part of the landscape.
