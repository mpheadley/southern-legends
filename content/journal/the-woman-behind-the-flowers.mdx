She was cutting stems the morning I figured it out.

Six o'clock. The kitchen still dark. Coffee going. And she was already at the table with the bucket and the shears, trimming ranunculus at an angle the way she does, the way I've watched her do it for years, and I thought: *I wrote that down somewhere.*

I went and found the notebook. Old one, spiral bound. Near the back, in handwriting I barely recognized as mine, I had written:

*My rock, my flower. You saved me, by the hour.*

I stood there for a minute.

---

We were on the farm when things started going wrong for me. I had the energy of ten men and the judgment of none. I was making decisions I wouldn't have made in a healthier state — financial ones, mostly, the kind that leave a mark.

Heather was recovering from her endometriosis surgery. She had barely gotten out of bed. And she was still the steady one.

That's the thing people don't know about her. She doesn't make a production of it. She just stays.

I got diagnosed. Got medicated. Got hospitalized — ten days at UAB. I came home to my parents' camper in their backyard, which is a specific kind of humility I recommend to no one and which I couldn't have survived without her.

She kept the kids going. Kept the flowers going. Drove an hour to see me with the kids in the backseat like it was normal, like her husband wasn't in a psych ward, like we weren't in the middle of losing everything we'd built.

She brought sunflowers.

---

We met when we were young enough to not know what we were promising.

She likes Shania Twain and A Tribe Called Quest, which tells you everything. She has a playlist that includes "She Thinks My Tractor's Sexy" and "Bonita Applebum" and does not feel the need to explain herself. The Dance Party playlist she made for the kids has "Beep Beep I'm a Sheep" and also Vulfpeck. Hannah and Noah and Soren and Sage know every word of both.

She is the funniest person I know. She doesn't try. She just is.

"I love you," she told me once, in the middle of a bad year, "but fuck you."

I said: I receive and accept that.

That's marriage. That's ours.

---

She runs four things now. Heather Florals, which does weddings and events across Northeast Alabama. The Bloom Bar, a converted horse trailer she takes to events where guests pick their own bouquets to take home. The Headley Flower Farm, which is smaller now, on her parents' land, but still going. And our family, which is the largest of the operations and the one she runs best.

She didn't plan any of it, exactly. She just kept saying yes to the thing in front of her.

Eyes like pearls. Hair just brown. Four kids who know what love looks like because she shows them every day.

---

I recorded a song for her on my phone. Old Coldplay, the one I used to play when we were dating. Cracked the window a little. Tried to hit the high notes. Didn't.

She's getting the real version next.

The one I wrote from that notebook — her words, her face, the thing I couldn't figure out how to say until I found it already written down years ago.

*You are my flower. You know as true. By whole life through.*

She was already cutting stems when I read it.

She didn't stop. Just smiled.

That's her.
