Anniston used to call itself the Model City. Tyson's Model City Glass kept the name and, in a way, kept the promise — it's the kind of hands-on, do-the-hard-part shop a real town runs on.

## Glass, and Everything Adjacent to Glass

At 101 West 10th Street, Model City Glass does more than the name lets on. Glass, yes — but also graphics, signs, aluminum signs, storefront graphics, and vehicle wraps. [VERIFY the full scope + how long the shop has been in business with Julie Tyson before publish.] It's run by Julie Tyson, and it's the sort of place that can cut a pane for your storefront one morning and wrap a work truck that afternoon.

The wedding piece is the smallest and the most personal thing they make: etched glassware. Mugs, pint glasses, and etched keepsakes — the kind of favor a guest actually keeps because it has a name and a date on it, cut into the glass instead of printed on a sticker that peels by August.

## Why the Small Thing Is the Whole Thing

There's a difference between a favor that gets thrown away with the centerpieces and one that ends up in somebody's cabinet for thirty years. Etched glass is the second kind. It's the same instinct behind everything else the shop does — make the thing so it lasts, so the town it's for can point at it later and say *we made that here.*

Julie brings the etched-glass work to The Aisle, and if you're the kind of couple who cares that your guests leave with something real, this is the booth to find.

## Why It's in Southern Legends

Because a glass-and-sign shop that has quietly lettered and glazed a town's storefronts is exactly the kind of business that keeps a place looking like itself. Model City. Still cutting glass on 10th Street.
