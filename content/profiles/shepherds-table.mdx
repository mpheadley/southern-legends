Every Sunday at 8:30 a.m., the doors open at 1431 Gurnee Avenue.

There is no eligibility screening. No intake form, no appointment, no documentation. You walk in, you sit down, and someone brings you breakfast. The Shepherd's Table at Anniston First United Methodist Church has been doing this for years — free sit-down breakfast, open to anyone, the focus being the people who have nowhere else to sit on a Sunday morning.

It is staffed entirely by volunteers.

---

## What It Is Not

The Shepherd's Table is not a drive-through. It is not a bag handed through a window or a numbered ticket for a line that moves toward a door.

It is a table. People sit at it. Someone sits across from them. The food is the pretext; the actual thing being offered is the ten or fifteen minutes of being somebody's guest rather than somebody's project.

That distinction matters more than people who haven't needed it tend to realize.

The campus at Anniston First is dense with ministry: Interfaith Ministries operates there, the United Way Warming Station at The Bridge is on the same grounds. People who live outside know the corner. They know what is available and what it costs — not in money, most of it is free, but in the dignity economics of having to prove you need it. The Shepherd's Table does not ask for proof. You show up. That is enough.

---

## The Summer of 2024

I was on staff at Anniston First United Methodist Church — ordained American Baptist, serving as Associate Pastor and Music Director — during the years the Shepherd's Table ran on that campus. I was also, in the summer of 2024, experiencing the worst clinical depression I have ever had.

I sat by the Camp Lee pool that summer surrounded by people and felt completely alone. I have written about this in *The Anniston Star* and on this site. What I have not written about is that I kept volunteering at the Shepherd's Table during those months.

I scrambled eggs. I poured coffee. I sat with people.

I was not there to fix anything. I was not there to do pastoral work — I was in no shape for that. I was there because some mornings the only thing that helped was having something concrete to do with my hands in a room full of people who needed breakfast more than they needed me to be all right.

The Shepherd's Table did not know it was doing anything for me. It was just doing what it does every Sunday at eight-thirty.

---

## The Logic of the Table

There is a theology to a sit-down meal that a bag misses.

When you hand someone a bag, you are solving a logistical problem. You are providing caloric input. When you set a table and sit across from someone, you are doing something different. You are making a claim about who they are — that they are the kind of person who eats at a table, who is someone's guest, who belongs somewhere.

The Shepherd's Table makes that claim every Sunday at 8:30 a.m. at 1431 Gurnee Avenue, Anniston, Alabama.

You do not need to be Methodist. You do not need to be Christian. You do not need anything except to show up.
