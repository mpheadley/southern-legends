Wedding photographs are relentless. They catch everything: the way the dress falls, the way the flowers are held, the way ten fingernails look in the moment the ring is placed. Most brides know this in theory and feel it viscerally on the morning of the wedding, holding the bouquet for the first time in the full light, hoping that the detail they almost skipped is actually done well.

Sy Nguyen has built a practice around making sure it is.

Nail work is one of those crafts that looks simple from the outside and reveals its complexity only when it's done badly. The polish that chips at the reception dinner, the shape that doesn't quite read in photographs, the art that was done in a hurry and shows it — these are small things that accumulate into the feeling that something wasn't quite right. Sy works in the opposite direction: careful, consistent, attentive to the specific outcome the client is trying to achieve. That reputation travels by word of mouth, which is how most clients find Sy in the first place. A nail technician who books bridal work needs to understand that environment — not just the aesthetics of the work but the logistics of doing it in a room where a lot is already happening.
