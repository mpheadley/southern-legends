Every May, Mickey Starling makes pancakes.

He makes them with the Kiwanis Club of Anniston, which he once led as president and where he continues to serve. They feed more than two thousand people. They raise more than forty thousand dollars, which goes toward back-to-school clothes for children in Calhoun County who would otherwise go without.

That is the number: more than forty thousand dollars, every year, from a pancake breakfast.

---

## Who He Is

Mickey Starling is an attorney in Anniston. He served in the Army — the branch that trains people to think about large problems in organized ways, to operate under pressure, to plan for outcomes that matter. He came home to Anniston.

He joined the Kiwanis Club of Anniston. He rose to become its president. He stayed on the board after his term ended, serving through 2028. He is the kind of person a civic organization keeps close after the title is gone, because the work he does is not about the title.

The Pancake Day fundraiser is the clearest expression of what he does. It is not glamorous. It is a breakfast. It requires a kitchen, a crew, a cause, and enough years of showing up that people know it will happen again. Starling has been part of making it happen.

---

## What the Pancakes Buy

The proceeds from Pancake Day go to Kiwanis programs that serve children in the community — specifically, back-to-school clothes for kids whose families cannot afford them.

Two thousand people. Forty thousand dollars. Children who start school in August with clothes that fit.

The math of civic service in a town like Anniston is usually invisible. You don't see the forty thousand dollars that came from a gym full of pancakes and a volunteer crew that showed up before dawn. You see the kid who starts school looking ready. The Kiwanis Club is the reason, and Mickey Starling is one of the people who makes the Kiwanis Club work.
