She was born in Montgomery and moved to Anniston in 1987.

She spent the rest of her life making the city work better for the people it most easily forgets.

Martha Vandervoort served as Executive Director of Interfaith Ministries of Calhoun County beginning in 2007. The mission of that organization is specific: *Bringing God's People Together to Serve God's People in Need.* She ran it. She grew it. She brought congregations across denomination lines into a working coalition around hunger, housing, and human need.

She died on February 28, 2015. She was sixty years old. Her memorial service was held at Anniston First United Methodist Church.

The *Anniston Star* ran an appreciation afterward that said the plain thing: *"Martha Vandervoort changed lives, changed Interfaith, changed our community."*

---

## The Chapel

Martha Vandervoort had a deep connection to Anniston First United Methodist Church and to [Camp Lee](/essays/camp-lee), the 382-acre camp the church owns on the mountain above the city.

After her death, the community named the open-air chapel at Camp Lee in her memory. It is called the **Martha Vandervoort Memorial Chapel**. Weddings are held there. Retreats. Formation days. People come up the mountain and gather in the open air in a space that carries her name.

[Kyle Bryan](/profiles/kyle-bryan) serves at AFUMC as executive director. His daughter Lucy, who died at three years old in a car crash in September 2020, was buried in a service held at that chapel.

Two griefs. One place. The mountain keeps what people bring to it.

## Martha's Hope

In 2022, the City of Anniston, United Way of East Central Alabama, and Anniston First United Methodist Church partnered to establish a homeless services center at 1400 Noble Street — at The Bridge facility.

They named it the **Martha Vandervoort Center to End Homelessness**.

It received $250,000 in state funding. It is still operating in 2026.

Her name is on a chapel on a mountain and a services center in the flatlands of the city. That is a footprint for a life spent deciding to show up.

## The Family She Left

Her husband is Dr. Kenneth "Buddy" Vandervoort, a board-certified orthopedic surgeon and partner at Anniston Orthopaedic Associates. He grew up in Guntersville. He graduated magna cum laude from the University of Alabama. He is a Founding Member of NobleBank & Trust. He serves as a Trustee of the University of Alabama System.

Their daughter is Dr. Lucile Vandervoort Morris, a family medicine physician native of Anniston. Their son-in-law is Dr. Andrew Morris, a sports medicine and orthopedic surgeon at Anniston Orthopaedic Associates.

The Vandervoort name runs through the medical and civic infrastructure of Calhoun County in ways most residents do not track. Martha's name is on the chapel and the shelter. Buddy's hands have been in surgery rooms in this county for decades.

That is what sustained presence looks like when you live it long enough.
