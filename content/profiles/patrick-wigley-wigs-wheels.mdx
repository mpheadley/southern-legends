We bought Heather's bike there. 2014. Hybrid cruiser — the kind you ride to the farmers market, kids in tow. Hannah was barely walking. We were still figuring out what kind of people we were going to be in this city.

Noble Street wasn't what it is now. The trail hadn't reached downtown yet. The extension was still a decade away, still a line on a grant application, still a thing city council talked about and cyclists drove past on their way to Michael Tucker Park. But Wig's Wheels was there, same address, same sign, Patrick behind the counter.

Trek dealer. Full service. The only real bike shop on the corridor.

---

Patrick Wigley was a U.S. Army medic stationed in Germany from 1990 to 1994. He had a triathlon coming up and no way to train for the bike leg. He rented a mountain bike.

That was it. That was the whole thing.

He came home from Germany, came back to Alabama, and started riding trails and advocating for cycling infrastructure while most of the county was still debating whether the old CSX rail corridor should become a trail at all. He's been at this since the early nineties — before the trail, before the races, before anyone called Anniston "Bike City."

He opened the shop in 2005. "Twenty years ago, when I started the shop," he told Rails to Trails Conservancy in 2025, "I knew the Ladiga Trail was coming to town."

He spent those years proving it. While the trail stopped miles short of downtown, Patrick shuttled cyclists from his shop out to Michael Tucker Park so they could get on the trail. He rented bikes to mountain bikers coming in for Coldwater Mountain. He showed up for the Cheaha Challenge, the Sunny King Criterium — on-site, on hand, making sure people who came to this city to ride could keep riding.

In 2021, when Anniston got $13 million in COVID relief money and city leaders floated finishing the Ladiga, Patrick told the Anniston Star: "Before they got the stimulus money, I was looking at 5 to 10 years before the Ladiga's complete."

He had been counting the years.

---

The extension opened in April 2025. $7.2 million. 6.5 miles, all the way to the Amtrak station. The shop that had always been closest to the trail's planned terminus was suddenly the trail's front door.

Rails to Trails Conservancy named the Chief Ladiga their Trail of the Month for June 2025. In the trail facts section — the list of parking, access points, rules — Wig's Wheels is the only rental shop listed.

On July 12, 2025, Mayor Ciara Smith hosted a community ride to celebrate the connection. The route started at Wig's Wheels, 8:00 AM, kickstands up. Patrick had more than 25 bikes ready to rent for anyone who showed up without one.

I was at the farm when they came through. Two hundred feet from the trail, post hole digger in hand, ratchet straps rigged up to lift a power pole. Smith called out my name. Patrick looked out at the pasture.

"It's looking good," he said. "You guys are cleaning it up."

---

Patrick leads NEABA — the Northeast Alabama Bicycle Association — and the Anniston Runners Club. When the trail extension came with complications the press didn't handle gently, he was the one at the community meetings, talking to reporters. Measured. Showing up.

A Jacksonville State study put the trail's economic impact at $2.2 million for Calhoun County in 2023 alone. When that number came out, Patrick told Rails to Trails he noticed other entrepreneurs finally scooping up buildings in Anniston. "A lot of the other towns that the trail goes through don't have the infrastructure we have," he said. "We have a hotel and a bike shop and restaurants and museums and other stuff to do off the bike, stuff that's now within riding distance of the trail."

He opened the shop before the infrastructure. He helped build the infrastructure. Now he's the infrastructure.

---

Wig's Wheels. 1229 Noble Street. [wigswheels.com](https://wigswheels.com). (256) 237-9447.

Road, mountain, triathlon, BMX. Sales, repair, maintenance, rentals. Whatever you need for the trail or just to get around this city.

He knew it was coming. He waited twenty years. It came.
