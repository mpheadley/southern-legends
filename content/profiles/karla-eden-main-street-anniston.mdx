Before Karla Eden had a title, she had a failed bakery.

She wanted to open a coffee shop and bakery in downtown Anniston. She tried. She ran into everything: permits she didn't know about, zoning requirements that weren't explained anywhere, leases with clauses she didn't understand, marketing questions she couldn't find answers to. There was no guide. There was no roadmap. There was no one whose job it was to help her figure it out.

The business never opened.

She could have left. She didn't. She became the guide.

---

## What She Built From That

In 2021, Eden joined the City of Anniston as an event coordinator. In 2022, she moved into the Main Street Director role. The thing she built there — the program that didn't exist when she needed it — is called GETUP Anniston.

GETUP is an eight-week free crash course for aspiring entrepreneurs. Accounting. Financial planning. Digital marketing. How to pick a brick-and-mortar location in Anniston's specific market. Every session is taught by an expert from within the community — real estate agents, CPAs, city staff — people who know this city's actual conditions, not a generic template from somewhere else.

At the end of the eight weeks, graduates compete in a pitch competition for seed funding to launch in the Main Street district. Main Street Anniston has awarded $20,000 to local entrepreneurs through the competition so far. After its inaugural class, the program expanded.

Main Street America — the national organization — selected Eden and GETUP for its Equitable Entrepreneurial Ecosystems (E3) pilot program, which came with a $15,000 grant. On December 18, 2025, Main Street America published a national community spotlight on her: "How Anniston's Karla Eden is Rewriting the Playbook for Small Business Success."

---

## The Numbers

Under her leadership, Main Street Anniston has produced: 70 new businesses opened in downtown Anniston. 229 jobs created. $71 million in combined public and private investment — $46.6 million public, $25.3 million private.

In May 2025, Main Street Anniston earned its first-ever national accreditation from Main Street America, the highest recognition level. It was the first time in the city's history.

In February 2025, Eden was appointed to the Main Street Alabama Board of Directors — selected specifically because of the results she had produced.

At the 2025 Main Street Alabama Awards of Excellence, Anniston won seven state awards — the most the program has ever won in a single year.

She also secured a $750,000 Paul Bruhn Historic Revitalization Grant from the National Park Service for downtown building restoration. Eligible property owners can receive up to $30,000 in subgrants for fire suppression, code compliance, and elevators.

## What She Did at the Market

She managed the Anniston Farmers Market. That meant overseeing vendors, managing the market calendar, navigating the city's logistics. It was operational work, close to the ground, with the same people she is trying to give a future to through GETUP.

The market and the Main Street program are the same instinct at different scales: building the infrastructure that lets local people build local things.

---

## Why This Is the Story

There is a version of downtown revitalization that is imported — the consultant who flies in, the developer who arrives with the grant already awarded, the program that looks like every other city's program.

What Karla Eden built is not that version. She built from her own failure. She knew what she didn't know when she was trying to open that bakery. She made a list of everything she wasn't told and didn't understand, and she built an eight-week course around that list.

The guide who needed a guide first is, in almost every field, the best guide. She is doing in downtown Anniston what downtown Anniston has needed: someone who came from inside the wound and built the thing that heals it.

Her own words: "Revitalizing a downtown is not just about restoring buildings — it's about restoring the heartbeat of the community, where history, innovation, and people converge to create a vibrant future."
