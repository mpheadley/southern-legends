I walked into the Calhoun County Area Chamber & Visitors Center to film Cher Dulaney promoting [The Aisle](https://theaislebridalshows.com), the bridal show I'm putting on this October. I walked out having been interviewed about birds.

<VideoEmbed url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hsUGoQUenHY" caption="Cher Dulaney on SkyTrail Festival, Calhoun County's first birding festival." />

Cher is the Chamber's tourism director, and she saw her chance. Before we'd finished the promo, she had me on camera talking about the SkyTrail Festival, Calhoun County's first birding festival, set for Oct. 9-10.

One problem. I've never been a birding guy. I don't own binoculars.

<Figure src="/images/profiles/no-binoculars-required/birds-of-alabama-field-guide.webp" float="right" size="sm" alt="Birds of Alabama Field Guide by Stan Tekiela" caption="Homework." />

Cher was unbothered. She had a confession of her own.

"I'm hosting a birding festival, but I do not know a lot about birding," she said.

I can relate. I don't know much about bridal shows either, and I'm hosting one.

Her festival and my show are even landing at the same place: the [Longleaf Event Center at Anniston Museums & Gardens](https://www.annistonmuseum.org), inside the [15 acres of gardens and trails](/profiles/anniston-museums-gardens) that draw people up the ridge above Anniston for reasons that have nothing to do with either of us. Two people running events outside their own expertise, at the same venue, taking turns promoting each other.

<Figure src="/images/profiles/no-binoculars-required/anniston-museum-natural-history-bird-hall.webp" float="left" size="lg" alt="Bird exhibit at the Anniston Museum of Natural History" caption="The bird hall at the Anniston Museum of Natural History, a short walk from the festival's Friday welcome. Photo: Jamie Martin, Alabama Tourism Department." />

That, she explained, is the point of SkyTrail. The festival is built as much for people who have never thought of themselves as birders as for the ones who keep a "life list" (a lifetime tally of every species they've spotted) in the glovebox.

That second group is bigger than I'd have guessed. A 2022 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service survey counted about 96 million Americans who watch birds, more than a third of adults. Alabama has built for them: eight birding trails with about 270 public sites. Calhoun County sits on the Appalachian Highlands Birding Trail, a nine-county route that opened in 2012. SkyTrail gives that trail a weekend.

<Figure src="/images/profiles/no-binoculars-required/skytrail-festival-logo.webp" float="right" size="lg" alt="SkyTrail Festival — Appalachian Highlands Birding Trail, Calhoun County, Alabama" caption="Calhoun County's first birding festival, Oct. 9-10." />

<VideoLoop src="/images/profiles/no-binoculars-required/longleaf-montage.mp4" layout="portrait" autoPlay={false} alt="Short video montage at Longleaf: the museum sign, the turtle pond, and the fountain" caption="The Longleaf campus: the museum sign, the turtle pond, and the fountain. Video: Matt Headley." />

Friday evening opens with what she calls a social sunset welcoming event at the Sensory Garden at Longleaf Center, 5:30 to 7 p.m. Registration is $25 and includes hors d'oeuvres and two drink tickets. Otter Creek Farmstead and Distillery will pour a bourbon tasting. Local artists will show nature- and bird-themed work. Alabama Audubon and the Alabama Trails Foundation will be on hand for anyone who wants to learn what they're looking at before Saturday's walk.

<Figure src="/images/profiles/no-binoculars-required/anniston-museum-gardens-fountain.webp" float="center" size="md" alt="Fountain and gardens at Anniston Museums & Gardens" caption="The gardens at Anniston Museums & Gardens. Photo: Jamie Martin, Alabama Tourism Department." />

<Figure src="/images/profiles/no-binoculars-required/longleaf-garden-entrance-loop.mp4" float="left" size="sm" alt="The garden path into the Anniston Museum of Natural History" caption="The garden walk into the museum. Video (cropped): Matt Headley." />

Saturday morning moves to Choccolocco Park in Oxford, at the Education Pavilion on Choccolocco Creek, 8 to 11 a.m. Guided tours come from Jacksonville State, Alabama Audubon and the Alabama Trails Foundation. Kids get craft stations, including one where they build their own binoculars. I could use a pair.

<Figure src="/images/profiles/no-binoculars-required/birding-usfws.webp" float="right" size="md" alt="A father and son birdwatching together" caption="U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, public domain." />

Then Cher shared the detail she clearly enjoys most. Birders have documented 188 species in the Calhoun County area. Choccolocco Park sits at exit 188.

"It's meant to be," she said.

<Figure src="/images/profiles/no-binoculars-required/festival-of-the-cranes-wheeler-refuge-mirrored.webp" float="left" size="lg" alt="Sandhill and whooping cranes at Wheeler National Wildlife Refuge, Morgan County" caption="Alabama's other birding festival: Festival of the Cranes, Wheeler National Wildlife Refuge, Decatur. Photo (mirrored): Jamie Martin, Alabama Tourism Department." />

By the end of the conversation, I'd changed my tune. I told her I was interested in coming.

If you're in the area and want the full rundown — tickets, the Chamber's own listing, everything — [Visit Calhoun County has the event page](https://visitcalhouncounty.com/event/skytrail-festival/). And if you're getting married next year, [The Aisle](https://theaislebridalshows.com) is at the same venue this October. Worth a look either way.

Whether I show up with binoculars, store-bought or construction paper, remains to be seen.
