There is a poem Brodie wrote about Heather. It is not my poem and not my place to quote it here, but I will say this: I used it in the Plainspoken Blueprint, the methodology book I spent two years writing, because the poem said something about a specific kind of love — the kind that stays through the hard going — that I could not say better myself. I asked Brodie first. He said yes.

That is who Brodie is. He makes things that belong in other things. Songs that belong in a worship set. Poems that belong in a book about staying. Coffee that belongs in your hands at eight in the morning when you are not quite ready for the day.

He works at Called Coffee on Noble Street. He teaches piano and worship and songwriting at Studio 104. He is a worship leader at Ecclesia Community. He is in The Evergreens, the ensemble that plays at whatever gathering needs music and enough heart to make it real. He writes poems.

He does not treat any of these as separate things. That is the most important thing about him.

---

Studio 104 is his teaching studio. It is not a franchise or a brand. It is a room where he teaches piano to people who want to learn piano and songwriting to people who want to write songs and worship leading to people who want to lead rooms in something larger than themselves. He is good at this because he does not separate the discipline from the meaning. He does not teach you scales and then, separately, explain why scales matter. The reason is in the lesson.

<PullQuote>He makes things that belong in other things. Songs. Poems. Coffee.</PullQuote>

He is a barista too. This is not a placeholder while he waits for something else. The counter is where he meets people, and meeting people is part of his work. I have watched him stop a whole conversation mid-shift because a regular came in who needed two minutes of actual attention. He gives it. The coffee is good. The attention is better.

---

He and Maria shut down a karaoke night a few weeks ago. I missed it. This is a recurring pattern in my life — I hear about the thing afterward from the people who were there, and I put it in my calendar to not miss the next one. They do not mind. They are both the kind of people who would do it again regardless.

<Figure src="/images/profiles/brodie-boyd/brodie-boyd.webp" float="center" size="md" alt="Brodie Boyd, musician and teacher, Anniston Alabama" caption="Brodie Boyd at Studio 104, Anniston." />

He is part of Ecclesia Collective, the arts community at the CAW Arts & Wellness Center on Noble Street. His presence there is not administrative. He is there because the thing they are trying to build — a genuine arts community in Northeast Alabama, grounded in place, anchored in people who actually live here — is something he believes in.

<PullQuote>He does not treat any of these as separate things.</PullQuote>

He is young. He is already someone who makes rooms feel inhabited. That is not a small thing in a town where a lot of the rooms have been emptying out. The people who stay and teach and pour coffee and write poems about other people's marriages — those are the people a place needs to keep being a place.

---

Studio 104 is taking new students. Piano, worship, songwriting. He is not hard to find. He is at Called Coffee most mornings, and the studio is on Noble Street, and the Ecclesia Collective is in the same building. He does not keep a lot of walls between his work. You will find him in whatever room needs him.

*Brodie Boyd's teaching studio is [Studio 104](https://studio104.gatherstudio.app) in Anniston. He is at Called Coffee most mornings. The Ecclesia Collective gathers at the CAW Arts & Wellness Center, 1400 Noble St.*
