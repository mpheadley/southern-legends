You drive down a dirt road to get there. That's not a flaw in the directions — it's part of what you're choosing when you choose Silver Run Chapel. The drive is a few minutes of separation from the ordinary week, and by the time you pull up and get out of the car, the quality of the air and the quiet have already started doing something.

Silver Run Chapel sits on a farm in rural Northeast Alabama. It is small on purpose. The kind of small where you can see every face in the room from where you're standing, where the person speaking doesn't need a microphone, where the flowers on the altar are visible to everyone because everyone is close enough to see them. That intimacy is not a limitation — it's the point. A lot of couples spend so much of their wedding day moving through a crowd that they lose the ceremony in the middle of the event. A small chapel venue doesn't allow that. The ceremony is what happens, and everyone there knows it.

Farm chapels carry a different light than churches in town. The windows face fields, not parking lots. In the morning, the light comes in low and yellow. By afternoon, it shifts. The setting is not neutral — it brings its own texture to whatever happens inside it, and what happens inside it tends to feel more permanent for it. That's the thing about getting married somewhere a person tends every day: the land has continuity. It was here before the ceremony and it will be here after, and something about that steadiness gets absorbed into the day.

Silver Run Chapel is available for weddings and private events. It is the kind of venue that works best for couples who know what they're looking for: something smaller than a ballroom, quieter than a hotel, and rooted in a place that has the feel of having been here a while.

The chapel will be represented at The Aisle: Anniston Bridal Show on October 18, 2026, at the Longleaf Event Center at Anniston Museums & Gardens. If you're looking for somewhere to get married that doesn't feel manufactured, it's worth a conversation.
