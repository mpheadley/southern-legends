When you walk the floor at a bridal expo, you expect flowers. You expect cakes and photographers and someone trying to interest you in a photo booth with props. You do not necessarily expect a children's book author. That's what makes Kathryn Parker's table worth stopping at.

Parker is the author and illustrator of The Goofball Series — a line of children's books built around the particular energy of kids who are a little chaotic, a little loud, and entirely recognizable. The series exists because children like this exist everywhere, and most of them will be at someone's wedding this fall.

That's the practical reality that puts Parker at The Aisle: Anniston Bridal Show in October. A wedding is rarely just two people. By the time couples are planning ceremonies, most of them are also navigating the logistics of children — their own, a partner's, a sibling's kids who are serving as ring bearer and flower girl, nieces and nephews who will be bored by hour two of the reception. A book travels well. It doesn't need refrigeration or batteries. It lands in small hands and stays there.

There is also something more layered going on. A marriage frequently creates new family configurations: blended households, stepchildren trying to find their footing, kids who are gaining a stepparent or a whole new set of cousins. These are real and sometimes complicated transitions, and children tend to process them slowly, across ordinary moments — a bedtime, a car ride, a Saturday morning when nothing much is happening. A book that makes a child laugh, that a parent or stepparent reads aloud next to them on the couch, is doing something quiet and useful in those moments. It's not therapy. It's just reading together.

Parker's books work as wedding gifts in a category that often goes unfilled. The registry covers the kitchen and the bedroom. Nobody lists children's books on a registry. But a flower girl who goes home with a book she picked out herself, or a ring bearer who gets to keep something that made him laugh during a long drive to the venue — those are gifts that land differently than another set of dish towels.

The Goofball Series takes goofiness seriously, which is its own kind of gift. Kids who are loud and scattered and prone to enthusiastic bad ideas tend to see themselves reflected in these books, which is the best thing a children's book can do. Parker will have copies at the expo for couples who want to tuck something into the planning that's meant specifically for the children in their orbit.

It's a small and practical idea. Most of the best ones are.
