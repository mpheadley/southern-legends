There is a bus on Gurnee Avenue that is not a bus.

It is paint on a wall. Sixty feet, maybe more. The Greyhound bus that was stopped here on May 14, 1961, and set on fire while people were still inside. Thirteen Freedom Riders. A mob with clubs and iron pipes. The bus burning in a field outside Anniston. Joseph Giri painted it.

He painted it for the Civil Rights Heritage Trail, for the National Park Service, in the place where it happened. The kind of thing that could be painted wrong in a hundred different ways — too sentimental, too brutal, too clean, too far away from the actual event. Giri painted it right.

---

He was born in Mobile in 1957. He earned a B.F.A. in painting and sculpture from Auburn in 1983. He has produced murals and sculptures in Los Angeles, Long Beach, Atlanta, Chattanooga, and Pasadena — including one at the NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory. He has been in Anniston and Calhoun County for over sixteen years.

His work includes paintings, sculptures, mixed-media pieces, and cast concrete with mosaic. The public murals are the most visible — the Freedom Riders bus, the large jazz mural near the courthouse, others across the country — but they are not the whole thing. There is a studio practice underneath the public work. Paintings, objects, made things that do not need a wall sixty feet long to do what they do.

<PullQuote>The buildings he paints are not just buildings. They are the places where things happened.</PullQuote>

The jazz mural downtown is the other anchor. Near the courthouse, building-sized. Anniston has been a music town in ways that the national story about this region does not always get to. Giri's work gets to it.

---

He was the artist in residence at the North Meridian Review in 2024. He is connected to the arts community across Northeast Alabama — the festivals, the galleries, the conversations about what public art is for in a place like this.

That question is not a small one here. Anniston has its history, and its history includes things that other cities would rather not have painted on their walls. The Freedom Riders bus is on the wall because someone decided it should be. That someone was partly Giri.

Public art in a place like this can mean documentation. It can mean insistence. It can mean: *this happened here, and the place where it happened is still a place where people live, and the people who live here are allowed to look at it.* That is different from a museum. A museum is a place you go to. A mural on Gurnee Avenue is a place you drive past on your way to work.

His website is [josephgiri.com](https://josephgiri.com). His work is on Instagram at [@josephgiriartworks](https://www.facebook.com/josephgiriartworks/). If you have driven through downtown Anniston, you have already seen his work. You may not have known whose it was.

Now you do.
