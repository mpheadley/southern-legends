The water on Cheaha Creek moves so slow it looks like it isn't moving at all. You can put a kayak in and sit still and the current will carry you anyway — a slow green pull under a full canopy, the far bank close enough to touch on both sides.

I have a picture from July 2018. One of my boys is in a yellow kayak, working a paddle that's too big for him, mouth open, all business. Behind him, Heather and one of the others in a red boat, keeping pace. The creek is the color of sweet tea in the shade.

Talladega Creekside Resort sits on Cheaha Creek at 760 Lake Whitland Drive, twenty minutes south of Anniston, off the same water that starts up on the mountain — Cheaha, the highest point in Alabama. The creek runs down out of the Talladega National Forest and past this bend, where somebody had the good sense to leave the trees standing and put in a few cabins and a floating dock.

It's a modest place, and that's the whole appeal. Over a hundred feet of creek frontage. Full shade. Enough current to float a tube from one end to the other and enough slack water to fish or swim. You launch a kayak here and you're on flat, easy water — the kind a four-year-old can paddle and a grandparent can enjoy.

<Figure src="/images/profiles/talladega-creekside-resort/creekside-creek.webp" float="full" size="md" alt="Cheaha Creek running past Talladega Creekside Resort, green water under a full tree canopy" caption="Cheaha Creek. The water starts up on the mountain." />

They rent cabins that sleep anywhere from two to ten, and RV sites with full hookups — power, water, sewer, WiFi — for people who want the creek without giving up their own bed. There's a clubhouse you can rent. Kayaks and tubes go out seasonally. It's open year-round.

The cabins have names instead of numbers — Old West, Old Glory, Farm House, Guest House, the Inn Stead. The Creekside cabin sits closest to the water. It reads less like a resort in the glossy sense and more like a family with some land on a good creek deciding to share it.

<Figure src="/images/profiles/talladega-creekside-resort/creekside-cabins.webp" float="left" size="lg" alt="Cabins at Talladega Creekside Resort under trees near Cheaha Creek" caption="Cabins with names, not numbers." />

Talladega Superspeedway is minutes away, which is how a lot of people first hear about the place — a quiet spot on the water to come back to after a loud day at the track. But the creek was here long before the speedway, and it'll be here after. It comes down off the mountain the same way it always has.

I don't remember every place we took the kids when they were small. A lot of it blurs. But some of it got a photograph, and the photograph holds the day the way memory can't — proof it happened, proof we were there, proof somebody was small enough once to need a life vest and a paddle he could barely lift.
