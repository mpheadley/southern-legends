Frank and Jane Brown are native Alabamians. They own 1,200 acres in Ohatchee, with 6,000 more leased around it. The property had been in the family a long time.

On April 27, 2011, a tornado destroyed 2,200 hardwood trees on their land.

April 27, 2011 is a specific date in Alabama. It was part of a tornado outbreak that killed 252 people across the state — one of the deadliest days in Alabama's history. The Browns' land was not the worst of it. But 2,200 trees came down, and they had to decide what to do with what remained.

---

## The Dog Named Merlin

The decision happened slowly. They didn't rebuild the forest right away.

Frank had been hunting quail — the old way, with bird dogs, following pointers through brush, the kind of hunting that requires both patience and landscape. He bought an English Cocker Spaniel from a breeder in North Dakota. He named the dog Merlin.

Merlin changed what Frank saw when he looked at the property. The clearcut land — the disaster, the fallen trees, the open ground where 2,200 hardwoods had been — turned out to be ideal quail habitat. The destruction had accidentally created exactly what quail need: edge cover, openings, the specific architecture of a wingshooting landscape.

They started rebuilding in 2013. Not the forest — the farm. Otter Creek Farmstead began to take shape as a luxury wingshooting lodge, built around the land the tornado had revealed.

---

## What It Is Now

The first hunt at Otter Creek was the 2016–2017 quail season, private hunting parties only. In 2017, Frank established the Otter Creek Kennels breeding program for field-bred English Cocker Spaniels imported from the UK — now recognized as one of the top programs for the breed in the country. The dog named Merlin led to a kennel. The kennel became part of the identity of the place.

Jane Brown is a talented artist, and her creative presence shapes the aesthetic of the property. The original family residence became the game room. The historic stables became the largest accommodation space. The place feels like something that grew from the land rather than something set down on it.

Activities include sporting clays, ATV and e-bike rides, fishing, culinary experiences, wine and bourbon tastings, and quail hunting in season. Accommodations include Hill Cottage and The Stables. The property hosts weddings — up to 350 guests outdoors, 150 for a seated dinner in the Grand Room.

The distillery is in development, producing "Fine Alabama Whiskey" in the ultra-premium category. Tastings are already offered on-site.

## Garden & Gun

*Garden & Gun* magazine named Otter Creek one of "25 Dream Trips of a Lifetime for the Traveling Sportsman" in their *Best of the Sporting South* feature in 2016 — the same year the first hunt was held on the property.

The magazine is read by people who have seen most of the good places in the American South. Being named to a list of 25 lifetime destinations in *Garden & Gun* is not a regional honor. It is a statement that the land in Ohatchee, Alabama — the land the tornado tried to destroy — had become something worth a trip from anywhere.

*Covey Rise* and *Absolutely Alabama* have also featured the property.

## The Address

Otter Creek Farmstead & Distillery is at 3165 Six Foot Road, Ohatchee, Alabama — Calhoun County, on the edge of what most people mean when they say Northeast Alabama. The name comes from the creek that runs through it.

The tornado came through in 2011. They started rebuilding in 2013. *Garden & Gun* came in 2016. The distillery is coming.
