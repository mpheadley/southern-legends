In 2019, Dr. Clinton Hubbard Jr. walked into Tuscaloosa First United Methodist Church as its senior pastor.

The congregation was 201 years old. He was the first Black pastor it had ever had.

This is not a detail. It is the sentence that puts everything else in order — the Birmingham-Southern degree, the Candler M.Div., the Gammon doctorate at the historically Black seminary in Atlanta, the years founding new congregations and leading Ethnic Ministries and planting Imani UMC and overseeing the Cheaha District. All of it arrives at that sentence. A 201-year-old Alabama Methodist congregation appointing its first African American senior pastor in 2019.

---

## The Long Road to Tuscaloosa

Clinton Hubbard Jr. earned his undergraduate degree in Religion from Birmingham-Southern College — a United Methodist liberal arts school in Birmingham with its own complicated Alabama history, which closed in 2023. His M.Div. came from Candler School of Theology at Emory in Atlanta, the flagship Methodist seminary of the South. His Doctor of Ministry came from Gammon Theological Seminary, the historically Black institution in the same Interdenominational Theological Center complex in Atlanta.

He trained at the center of Methodist institutional power and at the center of Black theological education. Both at once.

His early pastorates were in new and small congregations — Bethlehem and Holston Chapel UMCs, Mason Chapel, Douglasville. He founded Imani UMC, a new church plant (Imani is Swahili for "faith," a naming convention common in African American Methodist new church starts). He led St. Paul UMC in downtown Birmingham and Brownsville UMC. He served as Director of African American Ministries and then Executive Director of Ethnic Ministries for the North Alabama Conference — positions that placed him as the conference's chief strategist for reaching communities the historic white Methodist church had too often bypassed.

---

## The Years He Was Here

From 2016 to 2019, Clinton Hubbard served as Superintendent of the Cheaha District.

The Cheaha District is Northeast Alabama. Oxford. Anniston. Jacksonville. Gadsden. Talladega. Piedmont. Heflin. Every United Methodist church in Calhoun County, and the pastors serving them, reported to him. He was the bishop's representative here — the person who supervised appointments, navigated conflicts, and held the institutional weight of what the Methodist church was doing in this corner of Alabama.

During those three years, most people in Calhoun County Methodist life knew his name. The pastors certainly did.

---

## Tuscaloosa First, 2019

He left the Cheaha District in 2019 and was appointed to Tuscaloosa First United Methodist.

The Anniston Star reported it. CBS42 in Birmingham covered it. The story was simple and not simple at all: one of Alabama's oldest and most prominent United Methodist congregations, in a city defined by the University of Alabama and by its own history of racial violence, was appointing its first Black senior pastor after 201 years.

He did not describe it, in public, as a milestone to be celebrated in the way of a press release. He described it as work. He was right about that.

---

## Where He Is Now

As of July 2025, Clinton Hubbard Jr. is Superintendent of the East District of the North Alabama Conference — a new appointment, a new geography, the same posture. He has been doing this work for thirty years.

The through line is not ambition. It is something more durable — the belief that the Methodist church in Alabama should be what it says it is, and the willingness to occupy the rooms where that gap is most visible.
