A travel website will show you 847 resorts in the Maldives and let you sort by price. Ryan Worshek will tell you which three are worth the flight, which one has a reef that's actually alive, and which manager to call if your room isn't ready when you land. That's the difference between a search engine and a travel advisor, and it matters most on a honeymoon.

Ryan runs R&L Travel Advisors out of Alabama, working with couples across the region who want the honeymoon handled by someone they trust. He's spent years building relationships with resorts, cruise lines, and destination operators — the kind of relationships that mean something when a flight gets cancelled or a hotel overbooks. Those relationships don't show up in a star rating. They show up at 11pm when you're stranded in an airport and need someone to fix it.

The honeymoon is the first trip of married life. Most couples come out of a wedding weekend running on not enough sleep and a lot of feelings, and they need the next part to be easy. What Ryan provides is exactly that: a plan that's already thought through, with contingencies built in and someone monitoring from home. You don't have to read the fine print on the travel insurance. You don't have to figure out the currency exchange or whether the resort transfers are included. You just go.

There's a particular quality to the first big trip an Alabama couple takes together — somewhere they've never been, somewhere they chose because it represents what this chapter is supposed to feel like. Ryan has helped couples get to the Caribbean, Europe, Mexico, and beyond. He knows what couples from this part of the country actually want: somewhere warm, somewhere beautiful, somewhere that doesn't require them to make another decision for ten days.

He'll be at The Aisle: Anniston Bridal Show on October 18, 2026, at the Longleaf Event Center at Anniston Museums & Gardens. If you're engaged and haven't started thinking about the honeymoon, Ryan is the person to talk to first — before you fall down an internet rabbit hole you'll spend three weeks climbing out of.
