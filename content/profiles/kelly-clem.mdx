It was Palm Sunday, March 27, 1994.

The congregation of Goshen United Methodist Church had gathered for the eleven o'clock service. One hundred forty-six people were in the building. Rev. Kelly Clem, thirty-four years old, was their pastor. Her daughter Hannah, four years old, was waiting near the front for the Easter program.

At 11:39 a.m., an F4 tornado touched the roof of the sanctuary.

The roof shifted. It collapsed.

Twenty people were killed. Ninety-two were injured. Hannah was among the six children who died. There were no tornado sirens in the county then. No warning. One moment the congregation was gathered. The next moment twenty of them were gone.

A reporter found Kelly Clem the next day and asked her the only question reporters ask in those hours: had the disaster shattered her faith?

She said: *"It has not shattered my faith. I'm holding on to my faith. It's holding me."*

And then: *"Easter is coming."*

---

## The Sunday She Preached

She buried her daughter during Holy Week.

On Easter Sunday morning she went back to what was left of her congregation and preached. She was physically injured. She read from Romans. She chatted over a microphone with the children who had survived, and handed out Easter baskets.

She said afterward: *"There's no place I'd rather be today."*

And on what the moment meant: *"I feel like we're like a symbol of hope right now."*

On the nature of faith inside catastrophe, the line that has followed her across thirty years:

<PullQuote>
You don't need faith for the things you understand.
</PullQuote>

## The Long Work After

Kelly Clem did not leave. She stayed in ministry.

She served Holmes Street United Methodist Church in Huntsville for nearly a decade. She moved into conference leadership. She is now a District Superintendent in the United Methodist Church, overseeing congregations at the level above individual pastor. This is the career of a person who decided, at thirty-four, standing in the rubble of her own church and her own grief, that the faith was still true.

Her husband [Dale Clem](/profiles/dale-clem) was also a UMC pastor. He would serve congregations including Jacksonville State University campus ministry, Rainbow City, Trinity in Huntsville, and eventually [Anniston First United Methodist Church](/profiles/anniston-first-umc), where he served as senior pastor before his retirement in 2020.

Their daughter Hannah would have been thirty-six years old in 2026.

## What Goshen Became

The Goshen UMC tornado is covered in NBC News archives, in CBS footage, in the Washington Post, in NPR. Every spring-storm anniversary piece in Alabama eventually works its way back to March 27, 1994 and a pastor who stood at the edge of the worst thing that can happen and said the next holy day was still coming.

A stone's throw from the Calhoun County line, in a community most people drive through without stopping, a congregation lost twenty souls in under a minute. And their pastor came back on Easter Sunday and gave away Easter baskets to the children who remained.

That is the complete theology. The rest is footnote.
