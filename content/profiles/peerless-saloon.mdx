Robert E. Garner came from a farm in Georgia and opened a bar in Anniston in 1899. He went by "Daddy."

Liquor had been legal in Anniston for only nine years. The Peerless Saloon went into a Classic Revival Gothic building at 15 West 10th Street, and it has operated there, under various states of legal permissibility and cultural complexity, for more than a century. It is Alabama's oldest operating bar.

Daddy Garner bought church pews and sawed them in half for customer seating.

---

## The Bar from St. Louis

The mahogany bar came from the 1904 St. Louis World's Fair.

It was shipped to Anniston in 1905, reportedly on the same train that carried the Vulcan statue from Birmingham. The bar is still here — mirror-backed, intact, installed in what is now called the Alabama Room. The Vulcan is in Birmingham. The bar is in Anniston. Both of them older than everyone in the room.

Garner's house blend was whiskey called Old Wildcat.

---

## The Second Floor

The second floor of The Peerless was a brothel.

Four rooms with ornamented fireplaces. A fifth bedroom in the loft, accessible by ladder. Lucinda Talley was the madam. She operated from a chair at the top of the stairs — screening visitors, collecting payment, managing the floor. She carried a gun. At Christmas she gave expensive ties.

She ran it for more than twenty years.

Around 1919 or 1920, Anniston police chased a suspect into the building. An officer fired. He missed the suspect. The bullet killed Lucinda at the top of the stairs.

Staff and owners report seeing her there still — at her post, at the top of the stairs. Footsteps on the second floor. Glasses breaking behind the bar with no one near them. The Peerless keeps her story. It is, at minimum, the honest thing to do.

---

## The Dry Years

Alabama went dry statewide in 1915.

Daddy Garner apparently bootlegged until his death in 1919 at 63. The building went through decades of abandonment after that — one of those stretches of civic history that Anniston's downtown knows well, where a building survives its era without anyone being certain it should. The Peerless was restored and reopened in 1992. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1985, which helped make the case for the restoration.

Bill Skarsgard and Sebastian Stan filmed scenes for Netflix's *Devil All the Time* inside the building. The bar has been in a lot of rooms by now.

---

## What It Is Now

The Peerless Saloon & Grille operates at 15 West 10th Street, Anniston. It is a bar with a kitchen and an origin story that very few bars can match. The mahogany bar is still where it was in 1905. The church pew booths are a different flavor than they were in Sunday school.

The Alabama Room holds the bar and most of the history. Upstairs, they do not advertise the second floor as a destination. Lucinda is reportedly still there.

The address is 15 West 10th Street. The phone number is on their website, peerlesssaloonandgrill.com. Worth the visit, worth the conversation, worth knowing that you are sitting in a building that outlasted a dry law, a brothel, and a ghost.
