There's a certain honesty to a hardware store. The shelves don't pretend. The products are there because they work, because someone needs them, because a household runs on practical things — the right wrench, the right pan, the right set of hooks for the coat rack you haven't bought yet. Oxford Lumber's ACE Hardware on Alabama 21 has been that kind of place for the Oxford community for years, and when Bill Newman and his team decided to show up at The Aisle: Anniston Bridal Show, it wasn't a stretch. It was a recognition of something that most registry guides miss.

Setting up a home together is the actual project. The wedding is the ceremony that starts it.

Newman's ACE location carries the full range of what a new household discovers it needs about six weeks after moving in: kitchen tools built to last, small appliances that earn their counter space, outdoor gear for the yard you suddenly have to maintain, organizational systems for the garage, the closet, the pantry. The Calhoun County area is full of couples starting out in their first house or apartment, and the gap between what they registered for and what they actually need tends to show up fast. A hardware store, it turns out, is one of the better places to close that gap.

The ACE registry model is simpler than most people expect. Couples build a list. Guests buy from it. The difference is that the items on the list tend to be the ones that hold up — cast iron that gets seasoned and passed down, tools that outlast the people who bought them, storage solutions that move from apartment to house to second house over a marriage's span. There's a different relationship to that kind of gift than to the decorative ones. The guests who give them know it. So do the couples who receive them.

Oxford is one of those Alabama cities that doesn't make a lot of noise about itself. It sits between Anniston and Jacksonville, close enough to the Cheaha foothills to feel the elevation, and it runs on the kind of working-class practicality that built most of the South. Oxford Lumber has been part of that fabric, and the ACE affiliation gives them the inventory reach to stock the national brands alongside the local expertise to help people figure out what they actually need. That combination — national selection, local guidance — is what makes a neighborhood hardware store different from ordering something off a screen.

At the expo, Newman and his team will be talking to couples who are, in many cases, standing at the beginning of the longest project of their lives. Building a home — not the structure, but the daily life inside it — takes tools. Some of those tools are literal. A good set of them is one of the better gifts a wedding guest can give. Oxford Lumber's ACE Hardware is there to make that case, and to help couples register for the things that will still be useful on their tenth anniversary.
