Anniston calls itself the Model City. It also calls itself the Iron City — the name that stuck from its industrial origin, when the city was built specifically to produce iron and steel in the years after the Civil War.

Katelyn took the second name for her flower farm. Iron City Bloomery. The collision of those two words — iron and bloom — is the whole story of what is happening on Noble Street right now.

The city that made iron is growing flowers. The woman who runs Iron City Bloomery is part of that transition.

---

## The Farm

Iron City Bloomery is both a florist and a flower farm. Katelyn grows locally, which means the stems she designs with are the stems she tended. That is a specific kind of knowledge — you know the plant differently when you watched it come up.

She does weddings. She does events. She does what a working florist in Northeast Alabama does, which is show up for the most important days in people's lives with something beautiful in her hands that she made from things she grew.

---

## Noble Street

Anniston's Noble Street has been in the middle of a long conversation about what it wants to become. The storefronts are old. Many of them are still empty. But there are people choosing to be there — opening shops and studios and bars and bakeries in the buildings that used to hold something else.

Iron City Bloomery is one of those choices. Katelyn made it, and it is doing something to the block she is on.
