He came to Jacksonville State University in the 1980s. He got his bachelor's there in 1982, his master's the year after.

Then he stayed for thirty-two years.

He ran recreational sports. He ran alumni relations. He served as judicial coordinator. He officiated football games at the high school level through the AHSAA from 2016 to 2020. He served on the Jacksonville City Council from 2008 to 2016 — he was the first Council President after the 2010 Census restructure.

In August 2025, he ran for mayor of Jacksonville, Alabama.

He won.

---

## Town and Gown

Jacksonville is one of those Alabama towns where the university and the municipality are genuinely intertwined. JSU has been the largest employer and the largest institutional presence for most of the city's modern history. The relationship between what happens on the campus and what happens on the streets around it is not metaphorical — it is structural.

Mark Jones spent thirty-two years on both sides of that relationship. He knows how the institution thinks. He knows what it needs from the city and what the city needs from it. He knows the students, the alumni, the athletic culture, the academic culture, the way the demographics of the campus and the demographics of the surrounding community do and do not overlap.

Jacksonville elected, in 2025, a man who had been studying the town-gown relationship from the inside for three decades.

---

## The Guntersville Kid Who Stayed

He was born in Guntersville, Alabama — a town on the water in Marshall County, an hour north of Jacksonville. He came to Jacksonville State for school, and Jacksonville kept him.

That is a particular kind of local commitment. Not the person who grew up here and stayed because they never left. The person who came from somewhere else, found the place, and decided it was worth staying for. He has been deciding that for over forty years.

---

*Mark Jones is the Mayor of Jacksonville, Alabama, elected August 2025. He holds a B.S. in Education (1982) and M.Ed. (1983), both from Jacksonville State University, and spent thirty-two years in various roles at JSU before his election.*
