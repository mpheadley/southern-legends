In 1978, Teresa Cheatham won the Miss Alabama pageant representing Jacksonville State University.

She was a vocalist. At the Miss America competition in Atlantic City in 1979, she finished as First Runner-Up. She was the fourth JSU woman to win the Miss Alabama title.

Then she went to the University of Louisville and got a master's degree in music. Then she came back to Jacksonville and spent fifteen years doing public relations and admissions work at her alma mater. Then, in 2001, she joined the JSU Music faculty as a voice instructor.

Forty-six years after she first walked across a stage at Jacksonville State as a singer, she was still there — coaching the next generation of singers to do what she had done.

---

## What the Circle Looks Like

She is the assistant director and public relations voice for the Jacksonville Opera Theatre — the program Dr. Nathan Wight founded and runs. She is a vocal coach for JSU's annual musical productions. She is part of the voice faculty alongside Wight and others.

The Miss Alabama crown is not the lead of this story. The lead is what she came back to and what she has done with it.

She grew up in Wellington, Alabama. She competed as a singer. She won the biggest state title a singer from Wellington, Alabama can win. She got a graduate degree. She worked administration for fifteen years. And then, at a moment she apparently decided was the right one, she walked back into a music studio at the university that had given her the first stage she ever stood on, and she started teaching.

*Teresa Stricklin (née Cheatham) is a voice instructor and Assistant Director of the Jacksonville Opera Theatre at Jacksonville State University. She has been on faculty since 2001. She was Miss Alabama 1978 and First Runner-Up at Miss America 1979, competing as a vocalist. She is from Wellington, Alabama.*
