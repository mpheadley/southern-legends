Jim Williams came from Texas.

He was born in Mineola — deep East Texas, the kind of town that produces either people who leave or people who stay, and he left. He graduated high school as valedictorian in 1964. He went to Baylor on a National Merit Scholarship, graduated summa cum laude in Political Science, then went to Michigan on a National Defense Education Act fellowship for his master's degree. He spent time at the Texas Research League. He moved to Michigan to direct the Citizens Research Council.

In 1988, he came to Alabama.

Governor Albert Brewer and a coalition of civic leaders were building something new: the Public Affairs Research Council of Alabama, PARCA — an independent, nonpartisan research organization that would study how Alabama governed itself and tell people what it found. They needed a director. Jim Williams got the call. He took it.

---

## 27 Years

He was the first director. He was the only director.

For twenty-seven years — from PARCA's founding in 1988 until his retirement on September 30, 2015 — Jim Williams ran the organization that built Alabama's institutional knowledge about itself. School test scores. Budget transparency. Government operations. Teacher quality. Education spending. If Alabama wanted to know something honest about how it was doing, PARCA was often the place that found out and said so.

Under his leadership, PARCA won eight national awards from the Governmental Research Association for research quality. Thicket Magazine named him one of "Alabama's 40 Most Influential Non-Elected Alabamians" in 2008. The list is the right frame: the most important people in a place are not always the ones on the ballot.

His own description of what PARCA did: "We provide research that people and political leaders can use to improve public policy and government operations." That sentence is doing a lot of work quietly — the same way he did.

---

## What That Work Required

Alabama is not an easy state to tell the truth about.

It has a political culture that can be resistant to external accountability, a history of institutional failures that are sometimes more comfortable unnamed, and a public conversation that can conflate criticism with disloyalty. A nonpartisan research organization that publishes honest assessments of how Alabama schools perform or how Alabama budgets are built is, by definition, going to make some people uncomfortable.

Jim Williams built the institution that did that work for nearly three decades, kept it nonpartisan, kept it credible, and kept it funded — which is its own form of political skill, even if it is never exercised on a ballot.

---

## After PARCA

After his retirement in 2015, Williams affiliated with Akribos Group, a consulting firm, continuing work in public policy and civic research. He remains connected to the network of Alabama civic institutions he helped build over his career.

---

## What Alabama Has in Him

There is a particular kind of civic servant — not the politician, not the activist, not the entrepreneur — who does the foundational institutional work that makes everything else possible. Jim Williams is that kind of person.

He came from Texas. He built a research council in Alabama. He kept it nonpartisan for twenty-seven years. He never ran for anything.

The state is better documented because of him. That is not a small thing.
