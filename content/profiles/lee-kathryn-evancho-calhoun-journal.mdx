Local news is dying everywhere except in the places where someone decides to start something.

Lee Kathryn Evancho started something. The Calhoun Journal covers Calhoun County — the school board, the city council, the things that happen in a mid-sized Alabama city and county that would otherwise go unreported because the national outlets don't come and the regional ones are stretched thin.

The Anniston Star has been covering this county for over a century. But the Star is under-resourced, as every regional newspaper is now. The gap between what the county produces and what gets written about it is real. Lee Kathryn Evancho saw it and started a publication.

---

## The Pitch

The pitch for local news is simple: the people who live here deserve to know what's happening where they live. Their tax money is being spent. Their schools are making decisions. Their elected officials are voting on things. Someone should be in the room.

Lee Kathryn Evancho is in the room.

The Calhoun Journal is the independent outlet she runs. Contact is at the email on the masthead. She reached out about content partnership and syndication in April 2026, which is what local independent publishers do — find the adjacent things worth amplifying, build the relationships that make the work sustainable.

---

## Why It Matters

Calhoun County has around 115,000 people. It has Anniston, Oxford, Piedmont, Jacksonville, Heflin, and a dozen smaller towns and communities. All of those places have governments and school boards and businesses and disputes and stories.

What they have less of is coverage.

The Calhoun Journal is coverage. Started by one person because the gap was there and the gap was unacceptable.
