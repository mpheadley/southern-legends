Walk through the floor at a bridal expo and you'll see flowers and photographers and cake samples and DJs and photographers again and somebody selling honeymoon packages. Then you'll see Jonathan Land Roofing, and one of two things will happen: you'll do a double-take, or you'll immediately understand exactly why he's there.

The second response is the right one.

Here is what happens after the wedding: two people go home. Not to a hotel, not to their parents' houses — home, to the actual house they are building their life in together. And that house, whatever it looks like, sits under a roof. The roof is the thing that keeps the rain out of the bedroom, the attic dry in August, the ceiling intact through whatever northeast Alabama's weather decides to do that particular November. A good roof is invisible. You never think about it when it's working. You only think about it when it stops.

Jonathan Land is in the business of making sure you never have to think about it.

A roofing company at a bridal expo is either the weirdest thing in the room or the most forward-thinking exhibitor on the floor, depending on how practical you are about what a marriage actually requires. The flowers from the wedding will be in a compost pile by Monday. The honeymoon ends. The dress goes in a box. The house — the house is where the marriage happens, day after day, for as long as both people choose it. A roof that leaks is a problem that will surface on a Sunday morning when the last thing you need is a bucket in the bedroom. A roof that holds, done right by someone who knows what they're doing, is one less thing the marriage has to fight around.

The wedding gift registry has always included practical things alongside the beautiful ones — the stand mixer next to the good china, the tool set beside the linen set. What it has not traditionally included is home services, and Jonathan Land Roofing is making the case that it should. A gift toward a new roof, an inspection as a housewarming present, a line-item in a registry that says: we want you to be taken care of in the long run, not just on the day. There is something genuinely generous in that. It is the kind of gift that does not photograph well but shows up in ten years when the next big storm rolls through and the ceiling stays dry.

Jonathan Land does the unglamorous work. He climbs up onto roofs in northeast Alabama and finds what's failing and fixes it, and he does this with the quiet competence of a tradesperson who has been at something long enough to do it right without needing an audience. His presence at The Aisle: Anniston Bridal Show at AMAG in Anniston on October 18th is a small reminder that building a life together is not just a metaphor. It involves actual buildings, actual materials, actual work. And it helps to know the person doing that work.
