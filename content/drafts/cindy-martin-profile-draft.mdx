Before Alabama had a homepage, Cindy Martin had a slide rule.

She grew up in Bessemer, a steel town that knew what it was for, and she went to the University of Alabama and did the least likely thing a girl from Bessemer was expected to do in 1984. She got a degree in mechanical engineering. Two years later she added an MBA. Then she went to work for the phone company, which in the 1980s was the closest thing America had to the future.

That is the part people miss about Cindy Martin. Before she was a media executive, she was an engineer. She thinks in systems. And she happened to be standing in the exact right place when the biggest system of her lifetime switched on.

AT&T moved her from Orlando to New Jersey to help launch Worldnet, the company's dial-up internet service, back when getting online meant a screech and a prayer. She left to run sales and marketing for a search engine called Magellan, one of the first attempts to organize the whole web before anyone knew that was a job. When Excite bought it, Advance Media came calling. In 1996 they asked her to lead a new digital group in Alabama and launch something called Alabama Live.

You know it now as AL.com. She ran it, and the Alabama Media Group behind it, for sixteen years. A mechanical engineer from Bessemer built the front page that a whole state reads.

Most people would have called that a career and stopped. In 2016 she started over. She founded Bham Now, then The Bama Buzz, then Hville Blast, on a bet that ran against the current of everything else happening in media: that people were starving for local news that told them what was good about where they lived.

"There's no more exciting career than digital media," she has said. "It has fed my curiosity and insatiable appetite to learn. From dial-up access services, to search engines, to AL.com and more recently, Bham Now, Hville Blast and The Bama Buzz."

Talk to the people who work for her and the word that comes up is curiosity. She still wants to know how the thing works, whatever the thing is this year. That is the engineer, still in there, still taking the machine apart to see. Alabama has plenty of people who left to go build the internet somewhere else. Cindy Martin built it here, and then built it again, and she is not close to finished.
